Mortgage Broker Gold Coast Fees Explained Explained

Published Home Loan Broker Gold Coast material states that most residential borrowers pay no direct fee, because remuneration comes from the lender once the loan settles. That model is standard across the Australian broking industry, but it does not mean the borrower has no costs at all. The commission is built into the lender's economics, and the borrower should still ask how the broker is paid on the specific product recommended. For the broader context on choosing and working with a mortgage broker gold coast borrowers can review before asking fee questions, the parent guide covers the full comparison process.

The useful mental model is simple: the broker's payment is a lender cost, while the borrower's costs are the loan features, interest rate, comparison rate and any exceptions where a direct fee is charged.

The two commission types and what they mean for you

The published figures break remuneration into two parts:

  • Upfront commission: around 0.65 to 0.70 percent of the loan amount, paid by the lender on settlement.
  • Trail commission: approximately 0.15 percent per year, paid on the ongoing loan balance.

Trail is the reason a broker has a financial interest in the loan remaining suitable over time, not just at settlement. It is also why a fee conversation should ask about clawback and what happens if the loan is repaid early or refinanced shortly after it starts, since published material notes exceptions can apply and any fee should be disclosed in writing.

Seven fee questions to ask before you proceed

  1. Is any fee charged directly to me, and if so, what is it for and when is it payable?
  2. How many lenders are being compared for this application?
  3. What commission does the lender pay, and is trail included?
  4. Are you accredited, and under what industry body standards?
  5. How does the recommended loan suit my stated objectives rather than the commission structure?
  6. How is best interests duty applied to this recommendation?
  7. Will every fee and commission be provided in writing before I proceed?

The published FAQ stresses that borrower fees are the exception, not the rule, and that a written disclosure should settle any doubt.

Costs that sit outside the broker's fee

A no-fee broker engagement does not make the loan free. Borrowers should still weigh the comparison rate, which bundles most fees and charges into a single figure, and the feature set around it: offset accounts, redraw, fixed versus variable pricing, and principal and interest versus interest only repayment structures.

  • Low deposit purchases: the Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme is cited for eligible buyers without lenders mortgage insurance.
  • First home buyers: the Queensland First Home Owner Grant of $30,000 for eligible new builds under $750,000 applies to transactions up to 30 June 2026.
  • Investors: owner-occupier and investor lending sit on different pricing grids, so fee figures alone cannot be compared across loan purposes.

Reading the disclosure conversation against best interests duty

Best interests duty sits underneath every fee discussion. The published material states brokers must operate under the ASIC mortgage broker Best Interests Duty, meaning the borrower's interests come first when a loan is recommended. In practice, that duty is the check on commission-driven advice: a broker must justify a recommendation against your objectives, not against the payment attached to it.

A borrower who asks for written disclosure, a panel count and the reasoning behind a recommendation is doing exactly what the duty expects. If the answers stay vague, that is information in itself.

Who this fee guide applies to

This breakdown suits Gold Coast borrowers who want the fee and commission layer made explicit before engaging a broker: first home buyers weighing grants and deposit pathways, refinancers checking whether a switch genuinely improves position, investors comparing pricing grids, low deposit applicants, and self-employed borrowers whose income evidence can narrow lender options.

It is less relevant if you already hold formal approval and have compared the lender's full costs and features, or if you need legal, tax or financial advice, which this kind of guide is not.

  1. Frame the fee question early. Ask whether any fee is charged directly to you, and request the answer in writing before proceeding.
  2. Ask for the commission breakdown. Request the upfront and trail figures on the recommended loan so you can weigh them against the written disclosure.
  3. Test the recommendation. Ask why the loan suits your objectives, how many lenders were compared and how best interests duty was applied.
  4. Compare the full cost picture. Read the comparison rate alongside offset, redraw, fixed and variable structures rather than the headline rate alone.
  5. Confirm any exceptions. For complex or commercial lending, confirm any fee and the reason for it in writing before signing anything.
